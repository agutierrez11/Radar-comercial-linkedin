# Redesign a Deck like a World-Class Keynote Design Lead

> One idea per slide — cut copy by 60-80%, keep 50% of every slide empty.

![keynote-redesign-polish-deck methodology illustration](https://cdn1.genspark.ai/user-upload-image/slide_agent/v2-catalog-hero/142-apple-keynote-subtractive.png)

## Why this skill works

- **The audience stops checking email by slide 3.** A redesigned slide is a single word, a single image, or a single chart — the speaker carries the story; the deck stops competing for attention.
- **Same deck, half the copy, twice the comprehension.** Documented before/after studies of *Presentation Zen* polish passes routinely show 70-90% copy reduction with stronger recall.
- **You bring the deck you already have.** Drop in the existing PPTX and the agent returns a redesigned version of the same length or shorter, with a per-slide diet log showing every cut.

## Methodology cheat-sheet

**Presentation Zen × Flagship-keynote canon × build-don't-dump** — Garr Reynolds' codification of the flagship-keynote presentation aesthetic, applied as a surgical pass on an existing deck rather than as a fresh build.

1. **One idea per slide.** Reynolds' central rule, derived from frame-by-frame analysis of Steve Jobs' MacWorld keynotes[^3][^4]. The most common deck failure is two ideas glued together; the fix is always to split, never to compress.

2. **Visuals, not bullets.** Reynolds' *Presentation Zen* principle: "slides are slides; documents are documents; they aren't the same thing"[^5]. Bulleted feature lists are documents masquerading as slides. The redesign replaces bullets with a single image, a single chart, or a single number at scale.

3. **The Jobs 2007 keynote as reference.** The 2007 iPhone keynote[^1] shows the build-don't-dump pattern in operation: feature reveals are sequenced across multiple slides with builds, not compressed onto one feature-list slide. The redesign replicates this sequence pattern when a multi-bullet input slide is encountered.

4. **50% negative space minimum.** Flagship-grade keynote slides routinely allocate half the canvas to black or white space. The compression is the design — the empty pixels carry meaning. Decks that fill the canvas are flagged.

5. **Two type weights maximum.** Flagship keynotes use a single type family at two weights (e.g. SF Pro Display + SF Pro Text)[^2]; never more. This is also the Vignelli restraint discipline[^6], borrowed from print design — typographic restraint is the load-bearing brand cue.

6. **Slides are not documents.** The skill explicitly does not produce a document — a board pre-read or an investor data room needs a different artifact. Redesigned slides assume a speaker carries the narrative.

## What this skill produces

A redesigned slide deck the same length as the input or shorter, at 1920×1080, with each slide rebuilt to flagship-keynote standard: one idea, one verb, one image (or one chart), at most 15 words, with 50% of the canvas left intentionally empty. The output also includes a **Slide Diet Tracker** rendered at the back of the deck — a row per input slide naming the original word count, the redesigned word count, and the design move applied ("bullet list → single 200pt number", "stock photo → product on black", "merged three sub-bullets into one full-sentence title").

The deck enforces six non-negotiable disciplines drawn from Garr Reynolds' *Presentation Zen*[^3][^4], the 2007 iPhone keynote as a historical reference[^1], and the broader flagship-keynote design canon[^1][^2]:

1. **One idea per slide.** If a slide contains two ideas, the agent splits it rather than compresses it.
2. **One verb per slide.** Every redesigned slide names a single action the audience should take ("buy this", "look at this chart", "remember this number").
3. **Visual evidence, not bullets.** Bulleted feature lists are replaced with a single image, a single chart, or a single number at 200pt.
4. **50% negative space minimum.** The compression is the design — half the canvas stays empty.
5. **One type family, two weights maximum.** SF Pro Display + SF Pro Text (or Inter Display + Inter Text). Decks with three or more weights are flagged.
6. **Build, don't dump.** Multi-element slides reveal one element at a time; a six-bullet slide becomes three slides with builds, not one slide with smaller text.
