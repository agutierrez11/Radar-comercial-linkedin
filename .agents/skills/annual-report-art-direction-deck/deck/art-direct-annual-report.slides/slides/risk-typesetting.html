Risk 02.07 — Regulatory
If we lose our money-transmitter license in any of our top-five corridors, revenue could decline materially.
We hold money-transmitter or equivalent licenses in 41 jurisdictions. Our top-five corridors — BRL, NGN, INR, MXN, IDR — represent 68% of FY25 Total Payment Volume.

Loss, suspension, or material conditioning of any such license would, depending on corridor, suspend new on-boarding for 30–180 days and require migration of existing settlement to a sponsor bank arrangement.
We estimate revenue impact, on a single-corridor basis, of between 8% (Indonesia) and 17% (Brazil) of consolidated annualised revenue, before mitigation.

Two of our top-five corridors are subject to active regulatory consultations, the outcome of which we cannot predict and which, if adverse, would constitute a material change in operating conditions.

How the small print sits on the page
i. Risk heading is a sentence. Not a noun-phrase. Cooley's "if-then" pattern, set in display serif — the reader sees the consequence first.[8]
ii. Two columns, never one. Even on a 60-char measure, two 40-char columns scan faster on a phone screen than a single river of small type.
iii. One numeral per paragraph. Accent-red. The reader's eye stops on the magnitude; the legal team writes the surrounding sentence.
iv. Risks are numbered 01.NN. Section + sequence. Re-orderable in proofs without renumbering every cross-reference.
v. Pinterest 2019 is the benchmark for narrative-led S-1 typesetting.[4][5]
